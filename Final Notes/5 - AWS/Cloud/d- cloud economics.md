**Free Tier** – A limited amount of usage (compute, storage, etc.) offered free each month, mainly for new users to try services or run small workloads at no cost.


![[Pasted image 20260805093028.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260805093131.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260805093155.png]]
**On-demand** – Pay-as-you-go pricing with no upfront commitment or long-term contract. You pay only for what you use, by the hour/second, making it flexible but generally the most expensive rate per unit.


![[Pasted image 20260805093217.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260805093259.png]]
**Reservations** – Commit to using a resource (like an EC2 instance) for a 1- or 3-year term in exchange for a significant discount (up to ~75%) compared to on-demand pricing. Best for predictable, steady-state workloads.

 ![[Pasted image 20260805093336.png]]
 ![[Pasted image 20260805093352.png]]
 ![[Pasted image 20260805093411.png]]
The more you use/spend, the lower your effective unit price becomes — AWS automatically applies tiered discounts as usage scales up (common in services like S3).

![[Pasted image 20260805093612.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260805093756.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260805093916.png]]
**Price Drops** – AWS periodically lowers its prices as their own infrastructure costs decrease (due to scale, efficiency, competition), and these reductions are passed on to customers automatically — no action needed on your part.
